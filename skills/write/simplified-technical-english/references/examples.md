# STE worked examples

Longer before/after conversions and the ambiguity traps that STE is designed to catch. All examples here are original, written to show the rules; they are not copied from the standard.

## Example 1 — A procedure

**Before (typical draft):**
> Prior to removing the pump, you'll want to make sure the system's been depressurized, and once that's done the two retaining bolts can be loosened and the pump carefully extracted while avoiding damage to the adjacent wiring harness.

Problems: "prior to," contraction, present perfect ("been depressurized"), passive ("bolts can be loosened," "pump extracted"), "-ing" as verb ("removing," "avoiding"), multiple instructions in one 40-word sentence, "adjacent" (use as-is if approved, but the sentence is the issue).

**After (STE):**
> Before you remove the pump, make sure that the system is depressurized.
> 1. Loosen the two retaining bolts.
> 2. Remove the pump.
>
> Caution: Do not touch the wiring harness. Damage to the wiring harness can occur.

Notes: split into one action per step, imperative and active, simple tenses, condition first, hazard moved into a caution that leads with the command.

## Example 2 — Descriptive text

**Before:**
> The hydraulic system, which is comprised of a reservoir, a pump, and various actuators, is responsible for providing pressurized fluid that is utilized to operate the landing gear as well as being employed by the braking subsystem.

Problems: 33 words (over the 25 limit for descriptive), passive with a known agent, "utilized"/"employed," "-ing" ("providing," "being"), long clause chain.

**After:**
> The hydraulic system has a reservoir, a pump, and actuators. The system supplies pressurized fluid. The fluid operates the landing gear. The braking subsystem also uses this fluid.

Notes: four short active sentences, each under 25 words, one topic (the hydraulic system) held across the paragraph, "uses" for "utilized/employed."

## Example 3 — A warning

**Before:**
> Care should be taken when handling the coolant, as it may cause skin irritation if contact occurs.

Problems: passive/soft ("care should be taken"), "handling" as verb, hazard buried after the hedge.

**After:**
> Warning: Do not let the coolant touch your skin. The coolant can cause skin irritation.

Notes: command first (rule 7.2), then the risk (rule 7.3), active, simple present.

## Common pitfalls STE removes

### Pronoun ambiguity
Pronouns that could point to more than one noun cause errors. Replace the pronoun with the noun.
- Ambiguous: *If you fit the pins incorrectly into the seats, they can become damaged.* (Which — pins or seats?)
- STE: *If you fit the pins incorrectly into the seats, the pins can become damaged.* (or "the seats," or "the pins and the seats" — say which.)

### The dropped "that"
After verbs like *make sure, show, recommend*, keep "that." It marks the clause boundary and helps translation.
- Weak: *Make sure the valve is open.*
- STE: *Make sure that the valve is open.*

### Ambiguous "with"
"With" can mean "that has," "together with," or "using." Recast to remove doubt.
- Ambiguous: *Install the panel with the green fasteners.*
- STE (using): *Install the panel with the green fasteners.* → *Use the green fasteners to install the panel.*
- STE (that has): *Install the panel that has the green fasteners.*

### Telegraphic style
Dropping articles and words to look terse breaks rule 4.2 / 4.5.
- Not STE: *Remove filter, discard, fit new.*
- STE: *Remove the filter. Discard the filter. Install a new filter.*

### "Do an inspection of" (noun instead of verb)
Use the approved verb directly (rule 3.7).
- Not STE: *Do an inspection of the seal for damage.*
- STE: *Inspect the seal for damage.*

## A quick self-check when converting a passage

1. Split every sentence with more than one instruction.
2. Flip every passive sentence to active (keep passive only if descriptive and the agent is unknown).
3. Rewrite every non-simple tense and every "-ing" main verb.
4. Put articles and dropped words back; expand contractions.
5. Swap habit words for approved words (see `word-swaps.md`).
6. Move conditions to the front; move hazards into leading warnings/cautions.
7. Count words per sentence (20 procedure / 25 descriptive) and split what is over.
8. Make every reference to the same item use the same name.
