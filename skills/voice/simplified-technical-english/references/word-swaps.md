# STE word choice — dictionary guide and swap cheat-sheet

## How the controlled dictionary works

The STE dictionary lists the most common words in technical writing and marks each as approved or not. The convention:
- A word in **UPPERCASE** is an **approved** word (with its part of speech, e.g. `USE (v)`).
- A word in **lowercase** is **not approved**. Its entry points you to the approved alternative(s) and shows how to rewrite.

Each approved word carries a specific part of speech and a specific meaning. Using an approved word in a different sense or part of speech is still a violation (rules 1.2, 1.3).

Two things override the dictionary in the writer's favor:
- **Technical nouns** — any noun naming a specific thing in a subject field, if it fits one of the 22 categories (see `all-rules.md`, rule 1.5). Part names, materials, systems, units, colors, etc. are allowed even if not in the dictionary.
- **Technical verbs** — verbs for manufacturing/computer processes, subject-field instructions, or law/regulations (rule 1.12).

So the dictionary constrains *general* vocabulary (the connective tissue of sentences), not the specialized terms for the equipment itself.

When you are unsure whether a general word is approved, flag it for verification rather than guessing — this cheat-sheet is a small curated subset of ~2,000 entries.

## The most useful swaps

## Phrasal verbs → single verbs (rule 9.3)

Do not build two-word verbs. Replace with one approved verb:
- set up / assemble → **assemble**
- carry out / perform → **do**
- put in / fit → **install**
- take out → **remove**
- turn on / switch on → **set ... to on** (recast, e.g. "Set the switch to ON")
- turn off / switch off → **set ... to off**
- find out → **get** (the information) / **examine**

## The 22 technical-noun categories (for deciding if an unlisted noun is allowed)

Official parts info; vehicles/machines and locations on them; tools and support equipment; materials, consumables, waste; facilities, infrastructure, logistics; systems/components/circuits and their parts; math/science/engineering terms and formulas; navigation and geographic terms; numbers, units, time and symbols; quoted text; roles/individuals/groups/organizations/geopolitical entities; parts of the body; personal effects, food, drink; medical terms; official documents/standards/guidelines; environmental and operational conditions; colors; damage terms; computing/ICT; civil and military operations; law and regulations; animals, plants, and other life forms.

## The 4 technical-verb categories

Manufacturing processes; computer processes and applications; instructions/information for a subject field; law and regulations.

If a specialized term fits one of these categories, it is allowed even though it is not in the dictionary — but still keep it short, standard for the industry, and consistent (rules 1.8–1.11).
